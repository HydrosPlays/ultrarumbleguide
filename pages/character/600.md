Tsuyu Asui (Hero Name: Froppy) is a rapid-class character with a very supportive kit and high mobility. Coming with a short cooldown instant revive and grapple-type alpha. Her gameplay is mostly based on positioning yourself close to allies' fights and using your kit to contribute damage to your teammates' combos by either enabling or finishing them, as well chasing any players who may be running from the fight, essentially avoiding any enemies from healing back to full HP and going back to battle. As she is a low HP character, you will need to be aware of your surroundings and decide if staying in a fight is more worth it than a strategic retreat to use any type of Team Recovery items and give your team a full reset for a better advantage. 

Her low level reliance also supports this style of gameplay, allowing you to level up your higher-damage teammates and any type of boost cards.

## Alpha Skill - Ribbit Shot
<p align="center">
    <img src="https://ultrarumble.com/assets/Character/Ch006/GUI/Skill/T_ui_Skill_Ch006_Unique1.png" width="400" /></br>
</p>

During Froppy’s Alpha skill, she extends her tongue, being able to use it in two ways:

### Ground Alpha
When using alpha while touching the ground, this skill works as a normal beam projectile, like the ones from characters such as Assault Deku. Using grounded alpha will put Froppy in an endlag state where she will only be able to jump and use both beta modes. This means you will not be able to roll, melee/air melee, start the use of any items (except level-up skill cards), grab items, use another alpha, or activate gamma after shooting a grounded alpha.

### Then why is this useful?
Froppy’s alpha skill has a very long range and low cooldown, making it a good poke tool to use on enemies that are far away if you don’t necessarily want to get overly involved in a fight. However, you usually won’t want to use more than one or two alphas this way, since the damage it deals is not worth entering penalty cooldown. Usually, if you see an enemy being low health after your first poke alpha, it will be more worth it to get close with a grapple to deal damage from up close instead.

<p align="center">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HydrosPlays/ultrarumbleguide/main/gifs/600a-gif.gif" width="400" />
</p>

### Air Alpha
When using alpha while in the air, this skill works as a grapple, allowing you to latch onto surfaces or enemies and be propelled in that direction. This allows you to easily escape from conflicts where you are at a disadvantage, enter or start fights, get into close range (where Froppy deals the most damage), momentarily leave to use support items and get close to use your Special Action on teammates. While in the air after using an alpha, Froppy can also quickly change her direction with movement inputs, taking advantage of the momentum provided by this skill.

### How to use Air Alpha effectively
Froppy’s alpha skill starts charging back after a moment, even while mid-air. Since the cooldown is low, you will usually prefer to use the full distance it can cover so your alpha is back as soon as you touch the ground, allowing you to just jump and use it again without impacting your charges. If you want to go fast, you still want to use most of the distance it provides, but use another alpha right before you touch the ground. This allows you to get the maximum distance possible without touching the ground, making you able to use seven or more alphas depending on the height of the surfaces you grapple onto. If you only need to cross a short distance, you are freer to use alphas to change direction faster, even if you haven’t used their full distance.

## Beta Skill - Ribbit Swing
<p align="center">
    <img src="https://ultrarumble.com/assets/Character/Ch006/GUI/Skill/T_ui_Skill_Ch006_Unique2.png" width="400" /></br>
</p>

During Froppy’s Beta skill, she swings with her tongue either vertically or horizontally in a short range, again having two ways of using it:

### Ground Beta
When using the Beta skill on the ground, Froppy will perform a sideways sweep from right to left, dealing the most damage in her kit and leaving her in a short endlag state where she won’t be able to input any other moves except another grounded beta, making her unable to continue a combo off it herself. This is why this skill is used as a combo finisher for her own combos or her teammates’ attacks. Additionally, this skill can be aimed with your camera, making the attack go higher or lower depending on where you are looking, allowing you to hit targets at different heights.

<p align="center">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HydrosPlays/ultrarumbleguide/main/gifs/600b-gif.gif" width="400" />
</p>

### Air Beta
When using Froppy’s Beta in the air, she will perform a downward vertical version of her ground beta. After hitting this, Froppy will cause the enemy to bounce off the floor. After Air Beta, Froppy will be able to follow up with any of her damaging skills, including Alpha, Ground Beta, Air Beta, Melee, and Air Melee, with some being more situational than others depending on the height Froppy and the enemy have at the moment of hitting the Air Beta. She can also alternatively roll to quickly go back into movement to avoid getting hit by any nearby players when she performs this attack.

## Gamma Skill - Camofrog
<p align="center">
    <img src="https://ultrarumble.com/assets/Character/Ch006/GUI/Skill/T_ui_Skill_Ch006_Unique3.png" width="400" />
</p>
During Froppy’s Gamma skill, she will camouflage and become partially invisible while also gaining a movement speed buff. This state will end if you get damaged or use any skills or melee attacks. As Gamma conserves momentum while using it, it’s a great mobility tool, especially when mixed with your alpha momentum or for making you a harder target to hit on land.

Froppy’s gamma, however, has a startup as she turns invisible, making her unable to use any skills, roll, sprint, or cancel it by pressing the button again. This startup still allows you to jump while doing it. It’s highly recommended to use this mid-air to avoid feeling the startup at all, as it also makes you fall faster.

## Special Action - Froggy Catch
<p align="center">
    <img src="https://ultrarumble.com/assets/Character/Ch006/GUI/Skill/T_ui_SpecialSkill_Ch006.png" width="400" />
</p>

Froppy's Special Action (SpA) uses her tongue to pull a downed ally nearby towards her, reviving them, with the right tools giving her the possibility to reset a teammate back to full without giving a chance to the enemy to retreat and/or heal themselves, extending fights and slowly bringing them to your favour. 

<p align="center">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HydrosPlays/ultrarumbleguide/main/gifs/600s-gif.gif" width="400" />
</p>

### How to use SpA effectively
As Froppy you want to almost always try to revive a teammate, keeping close to any teammates who seem to be struggling in a battle, there's cases however, where you won't want to go for a revive and instead use revive cards to bring them later, to be able to tell when you want to do each thing, I have prepared a quick and easy-to-follow guide to practice and eventually be able to tell what you want to do yourself. 

**You usually will want to revive every single time, but in the following situations the revive could end up costing a match:**

- **If there's multiple stun characters in the enemy team:**
Do not revive unless you're sure you will survive the most damaging attack/combo they could inflict on you, or in other side, If you have seen them play and know they won't be a risk for you If you go for a quick revive and escape. 

- **If there's an AoE battle on your downed teammate:**
This is tricky, because it means your teammate will be dying quicker, you will want to revive from the place you will recieve the less damage/attention possible from enemy teams, If not possible you will want to locate the highest building nearby and latch onto it as soon as you revive your teammate, from this safe place you will evaluate if it's better to retreat or to use supportive items on your teammates. 

- **If you're low HP:**
You will never want to revive If it will cost your life during early or mid game, as Froppy will be able to collect revive cards quite easily with her speed without many risks, however during End Game you have to evaluate If your life is more valuable than your teammate's from a more objective point, for example, a good Young All For One will be more impactful in a small storm circle than you, lowering risks of losing the match as a whole. 

- **Is your teammate in immediate danger?:**
Sometimes it will be important to revive as fast as you can, but other times the enemy team will have more important things to attend before finishing your teammate, allowing you to heal before going in, save a SpA charge reviving them manually or even allowing you to distract/down them to allow your teammate to move away towards a safer spot. 

## Basic Attacks

 Basic attacks are one of the things you will need to master due to their high versatility. They are perfect for tuning activation and setting enemies up by catching recoveries with their tracking and ease of use. They come in two types:

### Ground Melee
 Froppy’s ground melee is a two-hit close-range attack where she first launches herself slightly forward with a kick and quickly ends with a dropkick. This attack alone doesn’t deal any important damage, but its tracking is what we care about. Thanks to it, the move is pretty decent at catching enemy wakeups to set them up for an ally’s attack. Even though they are knocked back for a short moment, none of Froppy’s own continuations from it are unavoidable, since she will take some time before performing the next attack.

<p align="center">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HydrosPlays/ultrarumbleguide/main/gifs/600m-gif.gif" width="400" />
</p>

### Air Melee
Froppy’s air melee is a lunge with a dropkick from the air that sends Froppy backward with a backflip. This attack knocks enemies back to the ground, making them enter a fall-like state where they will be vulnerable to attacks. You are able to aim this attack by looking up or down, which will adjust the direction of your lunge, making it go from traveling far forward to practically not moving at all depending on the angle of your camera. It can be used as a better wakeup catcher than its ground counterpart when mastered, and if you cancel the backflip by either attacking from a greater range or directly above the enemy, it will give you control of your next move instantly, allowing Froppy to perform continuations with no delay and making attacks after this one unavoidable.

## Techs
### Alpha Boost
By using Gamma after an Alpha grapple leap, you will be able to increase Tsuyu’s air movement speed. This doesn’t mean you will automatically be sent farther, as it also increases Tsuyu’s falling speed. Because of this, you will have to time your Gamma according to the jump angle and distance you need. For shorter distances, you will preferably use the floor, grappling as far as you can and using Gamma as soon as you can. For longer distances, you’ll have to use Gamma when Tsuyu hits her peak height. For medium distances, you will want to decide whether to Gamma toward when Tsuyu starts falling (farther) or before she hits her peak height (closer), depending on the distance you want to travel.

### Backflip Skip
As explained in the Melee description, when using Tsuyu’s Air Melee and hitting an enemy, she will perform a backflip animation, preventing any follow-ups of her own. However, by hitting Air Melee from a greater range, where Froppy touches the ground as she hits the melee, or from a greater height, where Tsuyu hits the top of the enemy, the game will skip her backflip animation, allowing for immediate follow-ups of her own, such as Beta or Alpha.

</p>


### Directional Speed
When using an Alpha to grapple onto a wall, you’ll conserve more speed and height by pressing two directional inputs at the same time, forward and left/right, making you travel higher and farther. This can be mixed with the Alpha Boost tech to make Tsuyu take better advantage of it.

### Jump Cancel
By pressing any of Tsuyu’s attacks immediately after a jump, she will perform their air version without leaving the ground, allowing Tsuyu to perform Air Betas, Alpha Grapples, and Air Melees immediately without needing to leave the ground. This means she can perform them immediately during a sprint.

<p align="center">
    <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HydrosPlays/ultrarumbleguide/main/gifs/600t4-gif.gif" width="400" />
</p>

### Air Melee Chase

By using the Jump Cancel tech with Air Melee to perform it on the ground, Tsuyu will be able to cancel the end of this move to immediately perform another Grounded Air Melee, allowing her to Air Melee the same target twice in a row easily if she is out of other options. More importantly, this allows her to chase close enemies by repeatedly cancelling Air Melees on the ground toward running targets. This method of movement is faster than sprinting, letting you combo roll spammers more easily while activating any melee tunings.

When drinking a healing potion of any type while using Camofrog, you will be able to crouch and hold forward into any wall or map obstacle, making the potion clip inside it and allowing you to heal more discreetly.

### Air Beta → Ground Beta
This combo is the most basic thing you will be doing in matches. It’s a simple, versatile, and effective option: perform Beta while in the air and continue with a Ground Beta during the enemy’s bounce animation. You can also add the damage from one or more Alphas used to get close and engage the enemy, making the interaction stronger, but since it is not a true combo starter, it has not been considered part of the combo.

### Air Beta → Alpha → Ground Beta
This combo can only be performed on enemies who are not touching the ground at the moment they are hit by the first Beta attack, making it more situational, but just as effective as any other combo. Perform Air Beta while the opponent is off the ground, then use Alpha while they begin to bounce. You will have enough time to finish with a Ground Beta thanks to the extra time provided by the opponent’s fall.

### Ground Beta → <a style="color: rgba(51,116,255,255) !important;">Alpha</a>
After doing a raw Ground Beta on GP, you will be able to follow up with an Alpha for a short combo. If we add the Jump Cancel Alpha tech to this, you can also use the enemy as a grappling point for movement. However, using this tech makes the timing tighter.

By using Alpha or the Jump Cancel tech with Alpha while looking diagonally upward after performing the Backflip Skip tech, you can perform this short and easy combo, which will also send you flying, as the Jump Cancel tech will make you grapple onto the enemy. Looking directly at them will send you backward, while looking toward their head or above will send you forward and upward. Movement inputs help with controlling this movement.

Tsuyu is a very fast but also very fragile character. When playing as her, there are three pillars you must understand: Positioning, Chasing, and Escaping. Tsuyu, both in aggressive and supportive gameplay, is a Silent Killer: a very sneaky and slippery character in which your ability to play properly around these three basic pillars will be the key to success. I will proceed to explain each one in more detail, even though all three come together:

### Positioning
As Tsuyu, you want to position yourself close to your team, but not as a shield or another backline character. You want to be at heights or behind map obstacles that cover you from the enemy team's line of sight, hiding yourself and coming out for fast attacks before retreating with your movement techs, only to come out from a different side. Tsuyu's preferred spot is above her opponents' heads. Take note that she is still a very loud character, so you will need to play fast around your opponents to confuse them as much as you can with this, playing her very much like an annoying mosquito.

### Chasing
Tsuyu is one of the strongest characters at chasing in the whole game, being able to chase every single character in the roster with the sole exception of Rapid Ochaco and Mirio's SpA. However, this is pretty well known, and Tsuyu is a fragile character, meaning enemies will hit you to get you off of them. This makes Tsuyu not want to chase directly, but rather from above or the sides, where you can still see and hit your opponent, but don't follow a straight path that they can use against you, making you harder to scare off. Do not underestimate any enemies, as most players know how strong Tsuyu is, even if she has little HP.

### Escaping
As said before, Tsuyu is a really fast and slippery character, which helps a lot when attacking, but also when escaping difficult situations. With her Alpha Boost tech, she can leave and enter fights in seconds. When escaping, Tsuyu is really loud between the sound of her tongue and camouflage, so you should try to use the map to break line of sight and be able to space out this movement more, running through some obstacles between Alphas. If you follow clear paths, the sound cues will easily sell your position against more experienced players.
